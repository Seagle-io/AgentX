Technical demo

The Creation of Adam,
rendered in characters.

Michelangelo, 1511-1512. The other three demos describe their painting by hand. This one could not: in a figurative scene, the likeness rests on proportions that no hand-placed shape recovers. So they come from the painting itself.

How it is made

Where describing by hand stops working.

A Van Gogh sky is a field of sine waves, a Hokusai print is a curve: you can write them down. Two human bodies, you cannot. The first versions of this page proved it — first a shapeless mass, then a retreat to the detail of the two hands alone. So the drawing comes from a real image, in the public domain, reduced to the resolution of the grid and embedded in the source. The constraint does not move: nothing is served, nothing is downloaded on load.

01

Two planes, not a file

Luminance at the full resolution of the grid, one byte per cell; colour at a quarter of that, the eye being far less sensitive to detail in hue than in lightness — the principle behind JPEG's chroma subsampling. Both planes go into a TypeScript module as base64: 36 KB of source, zero requests.

02

A palette by k-means

Each block of colour is divided by its own luminance, leaving nothing but its hue. Those hues are grouped into twenty-eight families, each declined in sixteen steps of lightness. That is what lets neighbouring characters of the same colour be drawn in a single stroke, instead of one call per cell.

03

The wash

A character's ink covers only about a quarter of its cell: a bright fresco, rendered in glyphs on black, falls to a sixth of its true lightness — measured, 42 against 158. So under each cell goes a wash of its own colour, with the character on top. The texture of the text stays readable, and the overall value becomes the painting's again.

04

What is still computed

The painting does not move; the animation sits on top of it. A bead of light travels back and forth from one fingertip to the other, a lamp sweeps slowly across the vault, and the grain of the plaster drifts. Three effects where only the phase depends on time — the rest is precomputed on resize.

Choosing the characters

Sixteen steps from empty to solid.

On a dark ground, a dense character reads as a light area. Density and colour therefore say the same thing, and the drawing holds even where the hue goes flat. One ramp is enough here: the detail comes from the image now, not from the ramp.
